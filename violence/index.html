01

Remitting Violence

Bishal Chalise

Abstract

International labour migration exposes sending communities to the gender norms of destination countries. This paper asks whether that exposure changes attitudes toward, and experiences of, violence against women in Nepal. I combine labour-permit records, which fix the destination composition of each district's migrants, with time-varying indices of destination measures against violence against women and of legal gender equality, in the shift-share design of Godlonton and Theoharides (2025): the treatment is baseline migration intensity interacted with the share-weighted destination index, lagged two years. Outcomes come from the Nepal DHS 2001 to 2022 (wife-beating attitudes of women and men, household decision-making, experienced intimate-partner violence), INSEC human-rights records on sexual-violence incidents by local government (2020 to 2025) and district-court cause lists (2016 to 2025). Estimation is by OLS with district and year fixed effects and district-clustered standard errors. Exposure to stronger destination anti-violence policy leaves women's attitudes unchanged and raises men's acceptance of wife-beating slightly, while reported intimate-partner violence in the past year rises. Administrative records point the other way: recorded sexual-violence incidents and rape and domestic-violence cases per capita fall, and divorce filings rise, consistent with changes in reporting and exit from violent marriages rather than in underlying incidence. The administrative families have no comparable pre-period and are read as post-period exposure designs.
