02

Gendered Diffusion of Norms

Bishal Chalise

Abstract

Does international migration transmit gender norms across countries, and does it matter whether the migrants are men or women? Using Nepal's labour-permit records to fix the destination composition of male and female migration from each district, and the World Bank Women, Business and the Law index as a time-varying measure of destination legal gender equality, I estimate the Godlonton and Theoharides (2025) shift-share design with male and female migration entered jointly: each channel's baseline intensity is interacted with its own share-weighted destination index, lagged two years. Outcomes are individual-level, from the Nepal DHS 2001 to 2022: children's schooling by sex, marriage age and fertility, women's employment, decision-making and spousal gaps, and, for 2022 only, mental health and menstrual restrictions. Estimation is by OLS with district and year fixed effects and district-clustered standard errors. Norm transmission runs mainly through women's migration. Districts whose women migrated to destinations with improving legal gender equality see higher modern contraceptive use, later marriage and fewer child marriages, fewer children, more schooling among young women, fewer menstrual restrictions and fewer depression and anxiety symptoms, while the same exposure through men's migration moves these outcomes little. Daughters' school attendance and women's employment respond to men's migration instead. The findings are exploratory and sensitive to the thin female-migration network.
