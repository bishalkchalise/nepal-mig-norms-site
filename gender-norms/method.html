Method

Nepal district d
destination shares sdp (labour permits, 2009–2012)
Qatar · Saudi Arabia · Malaysia · UAE · Kuwait · …  ×  destination gender-policy index Wp,t−2
share-weighted index Indexd,t−2 = Σp sdp Wp,t−2  ·  interacted with migration intensity MigRated
treatment: MigRated × Indexd,t−2
children's schooling → marriage and fertility → women’s economic and household roles → well-being and restrictions

A · Migration share Where did migrants from district d go?

$$ s_{dp} = \frac{\text{permits}_{dp}}{\sum_{p'\neq\text{IND}} \text{permits}_{dp'}} ,\qquad \sum_p s_{dp} = 1 $$
Window
2009–2012 (preferred); 2012–2015; 2009–2015  ·  robustness: 2009–2011, 2015–2019, 2001 census
Geography
75 districts (pre-2015 scheme)
Destinations
all permit destinations except India; male and female permits separately for the gendered design
Intensity
MigRated = annual permits per 1,000 residents (2011 population)

B · Destination shifter What changed in destination p?

$$ \text{Index}_{d,t-2} = \operatorname{std}\Big[\sum_p s_{dp}\, W_{p,t-2}\Big] $$
WBL composite
WBL 1.0 composite, 0–100 (8 indicator groups): preferred
WBL marriage
WBL 1.0 marriage group, 0–100 (marriage, divorce, domestic-violence law, household headship)
WBL work
mean of WBL 1.0 workplace and pay groups, 0–100
VAW composite
UN Women index of measures against violence against women in force, 0–100
VAW policy
UN Women policy-measures sub-index, 0–100 (laws, policies, budgets, institutions, monitoring)
Timing
two-year lag

C · Treatment Which Nepal districts were more exposed?

1 · where migrants go
$$ s_{dp} $$
2 · which destination changes the district faces
$$ \text{Index}_{d,t-2} = \textstyle\sum_p s_{dp} W_{p,t-2} $$
3 · how strongly, through migration
$$ \text{MigRate}_d \times \text{Index}_{d,t-2} $$

Estimating equation (reduced form, OLS)

$$ Y_{idt} = \underbrace{\beta_3\, \text{MigRate}_d \times \text{Index}_{d,t-2}}_{\text{reported}} + \underbrace{\beta_2\, \text{Index}_{d,t-2}}_{\text{index main effect}} + \underbrace{X_{idt}'\theta}_{\text{individual controls}} + \underbrace{\alpha_d}_{\text{district FE (absorbs MigRate}_d)} + \underbrace{\delta_t}_{\text{survey-year FE}} + \varepsilon_{idt} $$
Yidt
outcome of respondent i, district d, survey year t
MigRated
baseline migration intensity, annual permits per 1,000 residents in the share window; its level is absorbed by αd
Indexd,t−2
share-weighted destination index two years before the survey, standardised; included on its own so that β3 is identified from intensity × index, not from the index alone
Xidt
age, years of schooling, urban, wealth quintile (columns 2–4); column 3 adds development-region linear trends, column 4 share-weighted destination controls
αd, δt
district and survey-year fixed effects
β3
effect of one permit per 1,000 residents at a one-SD higher destination index; magnitude = β3 × IQR(MigRated)

Gendered channels (option in the explorer)

$$ Y_{idt} = \underbrace{\beta_M\, \text{MigRate}^{M}_d \times \text{Index}^{M}_{d,t-2}}_{\text{male migration}} + \underbrace{\beta_F\, \text{MigRate}^{F}_d \times \text{Index}^{F}_{d,t-2}}_{\text{female migration}} + \gamma_M \text{Index}^{M}_{d,t-2} + \gamma_F \text{Index}^{F}_{d,t-2} + X_{idt}'\theta + \alpha_d + \delta_t + \varepsilon_{idt} $$
Unitwoman / man 15–49 (DHS)
WeightsDHS sampling weight (v005; d005 for the DV module); administrative: unweighted
SEclustered by district (75)
Samplesmain; rural only; excluding Kathmandu Valley; post-window waves only
